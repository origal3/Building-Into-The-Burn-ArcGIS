 Building Into the Burn
### Population Growth and Wildfire Exposure in Colorado, 2014–2024

**[View the interactive StoryMap](https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/d1f409862eba49a894e2beb7ab1f88d0)**

[![Population growth and wildfire exposure index by Colorado county](Combined_pop_wildfire.jpg)]

## Overview
Colorado is one of the fastest-growing states in the U.S., and much of that growth is happening in the foothills, mountain towns, and wildland–urban interface (WUI). This project asks a spatial question: **are the Colorado counties gaining the most people also the ones where the highest share of homes face direct wildfire exposure?**

## Data
| County population estimates | Colorado Department of Local Affairs, State Demography Office | 2014–2024 |
| Share of homes in a direct wildfire exposure zone | Wildfire Risk to Communities, USDA Forest Service ([wildfirerisk.org](https://wildfirerisk.org)) | — |

## Methods
1. **Population change:** Calculated percent population change by county (2014–2024) and mapped it as a classed choropleth (−11.7% to +29%).
2. **Wildfire exposure:** Mapped the share of homes in each county located within a direct wildfire exposure zone (0.022 to 0.952, or about 2% to 95%).
3. **Combined index:** Overlaid the two variables to classify each county into one of three groups:
   - Low/moderate growth, low fire risk
   - Moderate growth, moderate-to-high fire risk
   - High growth, high fire risk (the "collision zone")

Maps were made at the county level (WGS 84) and published as an ArcGIS StoryMap.

## Key Findings
- **Growth is uneven.** The Front Range corridor, mountain counties, and Western Slope are growing, while many eastern plains counties are flat or losing population.
- **Exposure is the inverse of density.** The highest exposure runs through the central and southern mountains and foothills. Dense urban Front Range counties and the northeastern plains have the lowest exposure.
- **The overlap is widespread.** Most counties combine at least moderate growth with moderate-to-high fire exposure. A smaller set of counties is high on both, where development pressure and fire danger are converging fastest.

## Why It Matters
Every new home built in an exposure zone raises the stakes for lives, property, firefighting and evacuation, and insurance costs. The counties highlighted here are priorities for mitigation such as defensible space, fire-resistant building codes, fuel management, and zoning that accounts for wildfire risk.
